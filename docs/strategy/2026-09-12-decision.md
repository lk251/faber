# Faber's next commercial test

**Decision:** spend the next six weeks testing a small, paid, human-led acceptance
evidence review for one repeated software change family. Keep Faber as an OSS-oriented
verification toolkit with a possible commercial service/product layer. Do not commit
the next six months to a marketplace, universal policy generator, or enterprise platform.

This decision is made on 2026-09-12. It is medium-confidence about sequencing and
low-confidence about willingness to pay. Faber remains a pre-revenue OSS/research
project with a plausible bootstrap business experiment, not a validated business or
a demonstrated venture-scale company.

## Why this choice

The three upstream studies show that sophisticated teams already know a great deal
about validation. Hermes has granular CI and regression rules; Omarchy has task skills,
hardware/GUI checks and disposable-machine acceptance; nixpkgs has package and VM tests,
maintainer policy and base/head/test-merge binding. A strong agent can read these and
propose an appropriate checklist. Generating another policy file is weak differentiation.

The possible gap is operational: the reviewer must determine whether the required
scenario was actually exercised against the right candidate and environment, whether
skip/cancellation counts as missing evidence, and whether an incident's lesson remains
covered after later changes. That could recur often enough to justify help. It has not
yet been shown to justify a separate product.

The commercial landscape is less open than the old narrative implies. Qodo already
markets versioned, owned rules inferred from PR history; CodeRabbit offers custom
pre-merge checks and agent workflows. Reusable deterministic evidence could still help,
but Faber cannot assume exclusivity over policy learning or risk-based review.
See [commercial evidence](2026-09-12-commercial-analysis.md) and
[the three-repository synthesis](../research/2026-09-12-policy-study-synthesis.md).

## The first buyer and the exact job

Target a founder/CTO or engineering lead of a **roughly 5–30 engineer B2B software
team**, already using coding agents and an existing CI/review stack, with one active
dependency, framework, service-configuration or API compatibility change family.
They must own a discretionary engineering budget and be able to supply a public repo
or a lawful, redacted export. The end user is the senior engineer repeatedly checking
acceptance, release readiness, and regressions.

Prefer a team with at least several hours a week spent reconstructing this evidence
and recent examples of skipped checks, expensive reproductions, rollback or repetitive
review. Do not presume that PR count or use of an agent proves pain. Exclude solo
hobbyists with no budget, projects happy with existing checks, and enterprise-wide
procurement for the first offer.

Ask for **€1,000 for a two-week acceptance evidence pilot**: one repo, one task family,
ten historical changes, up to two owner-approved regression/check improvements, and
a plain report comparing acceptance evidence and reviewer effort with the current
workflow. A shorter **€250 diagnostic**, credited toward the pilot, lowers initial
commitment. These are price hypotheses, not quoted market rates or sales.

GitHub and the customer's CI remain their working systems. The deliverable can be a
normal policy PR, existing test updates and a portable evidence table. A new Faber
account, new record system, payment protocol and upstream maintainer participation are
unnecessary. [Full offer and first-revenue plan](2026-09-12-first-revenue-plan.md).

## Six-to-twelve-month direction, conditional on evidence

**First six weeks:** get a paid diagnostic, then a pilot; compare fairly against the
best existing workflow. In parallel, retain the research/audit assets. Cap speculative
code. If ten qualified offers yield no payment or active pilot commitment, change the
offer once based on recorded objections, then stop that wedge if another ten fail.

**Months 2–3 if useful:** deliver to three independent teams in the same change family.
Record actual reviewer minutes, integration hours, evidence gaps, false alarms and
maintenance. Request renewal before expanding functionality. Do not turn a bespoke
consulting result into a software revenue claim.

**Months 4–6 if repeatable:** productize only the repeatedly used portion: evidence
coverage checks, owner-reviewed policy diffs and CI artifact export. Keep the local
reader and policy format available for inspection; a commercial layer can supply
onboarding, maintained checks, support and private deployment.

**Months 6–12 if renewals hold:** decide between a profitable specialist service,
a conventional paid developer tool, and an enterprise expansion. An OSS distribution
strategy is helpful only if engineers reuse it and introduce buyers. Infrastructure
for routing and provider substitution follows acceptance value, not the reverse.

## Company classification and what would change it

No evidence yet supports pitching Faber as an already venture-backable company.
Venture potential is conditional on a repeatable control point across many teams,
renewals with modest service labor, material expansion across repos, defensible
integration/distribution and evidence that incumbents do not absorb the value.
Three paid pilots alone would support a business hypothesis, not venture scale.

A bootstrap business becomes credible with repeat purchases at positive contribution
margin after founder labor, low-friction onboarding and a buyer who describes the
problem without being taught Faber terminology. A research-first classification is
appropriate if users value the ideas but refuse to pay and no transferable workflow
advantage emerges. Licensing requires an actual integrator's need, not merely a clean
library API.

The current moat is modest engineering know-how and a useful failure corpus. The
potential data advantage is accepted scenario/check evolution across repeated tasks,
with rights to use those records. Public incidents are available to competitors;
private customer data do not automatically become Faber's training asset. No network
effect is required for the first wedge, and none is claimed.

## What to stop building

Deprioritize the old automatic no-money Hermes contribution, the arena, GitHub App
delivery infrastructure, marketplace/funding/settlement extensions, Episode Envelope,
rich training exports, learned routing, universal policy inference, production runners,
enterprise dashboards and vertical integrations until a concrete experiment requires
them. Preserve historical code and docs; do not delete them to manufacture focus.

Complete independent A2 verification before promoting existing Proof as authoritative
acceptance infrastructure. That gate does not block a read-only diagnostic or this
sprint's isolated experiment. No contest artifacts or audit conclusions change here.

## Highest-leverage next experiment and machine work

The largest uncertainty is whether a buyer will pay for repeated evidence completeness
after seeing what their existing agent and CI can already do. The next experiment is
an explicit priced offer using one real change and one concrete missing-evidence
example, followed by a controlled pilot. Desk research cannot supply that answer.

The justified machine slice is an **isolated, data-only acceptance evidence demo and
evaluation kit**. It makes skipped/missing/wrong-context evidence inspectable and gives
the pilot a reproducible input/output contract. It must not create an executable
catalog from repository text, issue merge authority, or claim measured bug-detection
lift. Its purpose is to make the offer concrete and cheap to falsify, not to establish
a second product. [Experiment protocol](2026-09-12-experiments.md).
