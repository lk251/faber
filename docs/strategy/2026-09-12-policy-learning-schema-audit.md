# Policy-learning data: preserve observations, reuse existing records

Date: 2026-09-12. Read-only code inspection at sprint baseline `2b61006bda7839fa0616a86ccbbaa96c6c1ecfc6`; subsequent implementation is identified separately below. This audit concerns learning which verification to propose, not allowing a model to decide authoritative acceptance.

**Decision:** do not build a new universal episode schema. Faber already has rich, bound planning records, proof results, traces, human feedback and data-rights primitives. The useful near-term addition is an opt-in observation journal that retains the exact proposal-time view before a planner can fail, preserves successive runs, and attaches later feedback without rewriting what was known earlier. Training is premature until a frontier model with retrieved, temporally valid histories is beaten on useful check selection per cost.

## Information lost or expensive to reconstruct

1. **Planning failure and refusal selection bias.** `run_proof_product` at line 1168 constructs the request, calls the planner, executes the workflow and only then creates/stages the output bundle. A `ProofPlanningError` is converted to a user-facing `ProofProductError`; this path does not publish the request or the model error record. A future corpus built only from published bundles systematically misses failed attempts to formulate policy. This is a persistence gap, not a missing request schema.
2. **Repair chronology can be overwritten.** `_publish_stage` at line 1141 atomically replaces an existing validated managed bundle and deletes its backup after success. This is correct current-result behavior, but successive retries into default `.faber/proof` do not produce immutable learning history. The original proposed check set, rejected candidate and timing can disappear unless the caller chose unique output directories.
3. **Actual proposal-time boundary.** `_attempt_for_context` at line 129 sets `Attempt.created_at` to `context.candidate_created_at`, the candidate's Git timestamp. `ProofPlanningRequest`/`ProofPlan` have no wall-clock proposal observation. Git timestamps and file mtimes cannot establish which issue, policy or retrieval result was visible when a choice occurred. Capture `observed_at` at the proposal boundary, with provenance describing the clock as local observation, not cryptographic truth.
4. **Retrieved-history provenance and ordering.** `run_proof_product` does not currently supply `file_summaries` to `build_planning_request`; the richer lower-level API can. No explicit retrieved-episode IDs, retrieval algorithm/version, temporal cutoff, candidate pool, scores/order or redacted retrieved text is part of the product invocation. If retrieval is later added, preserve the actual view and reference sequence at call time. Do not add a speculative retrieval subsystem just to populate these fields.
5. **Owner correction and rejected policy alternatives.** A final plan cannot reveal alternatives considered, why an owner removed or added checks, what budget constrained the decision or whether a costly check was unavailable. Catalog membership is only the available capability set. Capture explicit owner feedback with proposal reference and reason when provided. Missing alternatives, scores, propensity and counterfactual outcomes remain `null`/unknown; do not synthesize them from a later correct answer.
6. **Per-check utility versus task outcome.** Proof receipts preserve executed evidence; they do not generally measure incremental defects caught, review minutes saved, field regressions later discovered or cost of a waived check. Existing `ReviewFrictionSignal` and nullable reward/cost/latency fields are reusable. Distinguish observed success, self-reported usefulness, authoritative check result and delayed field outcome.
7. **Environment and permissions as-of time.** Source hashes are useful but cannot reconstruct a rolling package mirror, actual hardware, transient service availability, a branch rule or a then-valid consent grant after it changes. Capture approved references/snapshots only for the narrow environments relevant to a pilot. Do not copy production secrets or collect private data speculatively.

The [Omarchy chronological example](../research/2026-09-12-omarchy-policy-study.md#appendix-one-chronological-policy-learning-example) shows why this matters: its fresh-ISO policy existed before the incident. The later patch and merged test show a useful repair, but public history does not disclose all proposed checks, actual test runs, review effort or outcomes of unchosen policies.

## Existing export assumptions that must not become policy-learning claims

`router_training_record` in [`router_datasets.py`](../../src/faber/router_datasets.py) emits `label_strength="strong"` when the trajectory quality report is RL-grade. That is a record-quality classification, not a causal guarantee that the selected policy was optimal. Its `selected_verifier_id` is derived from the receipt; verifier selection quality cannot be inferred from having a receipt.

Its `_sum_minor_units` returns zero for empty cost metadata and its value calculation divides by `max(cost, 1)`. That may serve the existing demo export but is unsuitable for a new cost-sensitive policy dataset: missing cost must remain unknown, not zero-cost efficiency. Reuse nullable `RewardSignal` semantics instead. Do not change these existing digests during this sprint merely to fit a speculative training use.

`datasets.assign_split` hashes a record ID. It creates stable partitions but does not prevent leakage across adjacent fixes, same repository, repeated candidate or future incidents. A policy-learning evaluation needs explicit chronological and repository-level groups. Also, `Trajectory.learning_context` includes the router decision among input fields while selecting worker identity as a target; a policy-ranking experiment must design its own pre-decision view rather than feed the convenience dictionary blindly to a model.

## Smallest justified capture addition

Implement an opt-in local observation writer outside authoritative proof bundles, using existing `TraceEvent` serialization and existing request/result records. Default behavior and core schemas/digests stay unchanged. The writer may add a small documented payload convention without declaring a new universal protocol.

1. Before invoking a planner, exclusively create a unique run directory, save the validated redacted `ProofPlanningRequest`, and emit `policy.proposal_observed` with local wall time, request/artifact digest, current policy digest and explicit unknown alternatives/cost. A filesystem failure must be visible before the caller can believe capture succeeded.
2. After planning, save the validated `ProofPlanningResult` and emit `policy.proposal_result`; on a planner failure, emit only a validated stable error code and available safe outcome metadata. Never persist arbitrary exception text, raw provider messages, keys or private chain-of-thought.
3. Retain observations for repeated runs even when the normal proof output is replaced. Each event/artifact is created exclusively; use unique immutable event files if the current trace writer cannot append safely. Bound record sizes and reads, reject symlink/path redirection and overlap with replaceable bundle output, and keep in-repository writes inside excluded local state.
4. Provide a separate delayed-feedback helper referencing the existing proposal digest and optional review-receipt digest, with owner-reported decision and bounded reason **codes**, not copied private comments. A self-attested reviewer reference is not an authenticated owner approval. It cannot change a proof decision, register a capability or grant training consent.
5. Preserve failed and unselected actions as observations only when actually observed. Missing alternatives or costs stay null. Do not label archival imports with their reconstructed timestamp as if recorded prospectively; mark import provenance and the actual capture date separately.

The safe deployment boundary is local, opt-in, single-user observation. It is not a tamper-proof log, hosted data platform, tenant isolation system, model trainer or automatically consented dataset. Repository-owner policy and deterministic proof execution remain authoritative.

Acceptance tests should establish: request exists before a simulated planner failure; failure observation is retained without exception text; successive/repaired runs retain distinct histories; observations and owner-reported feedback do not change proof verdicts or imply training rights; oversize/unsafe/unwritable destinations fail explicitly; and no-observation runs retain existing behavior. Add a no-key reproducible example. Full regression checks still apply to any integration change.

## Implemented local capture experiment

The bounded experiment is implemented in [`proof_observations.py`](../../src/faber/proof_observations.py), with the optional `observations_directory` argument on `run_proof_product` and CLI flag `--observations-dir`. It reuses existing records without changing their schemas or canonical digests. [Usage and boundaries](../PROOF_OBSERVATIONS.md) document the payload convention and delayed-feedback helper.

The initial event retains the exact already-redacted request and owner policy before invocation, with actual wall time, requested model, live/replay mode and explicit dry-run flag. Planner results or closed failure codes follow separately; safe usage/latency from an error is retained only when its model record binds this request. Successful workflow observations archive the complete existing `ProofWorkflowResult`, preserving per-check evidence, verifier runs, receipts and timings after normal output replacement. Feedback validates the original request/result event and artifact digests and remains self-attested, with no execution or training authority. Both absent counterfactuals and absent cost remain null.

Native Windows extended-prefix and short-path alias rejection tests passed. Focused Ruff and mypy passed. Full repository checks are tracked in the [sprint handoff](2026-09-12-sprint-handoff.md).

The no-key script reported **3 retained runs, 9 events, 95,362 history bytes and 6.645 seconds**, with zero provider calls. It reused one proof output for the `BLOCK` and repaired `PASS` candidates, retained the deliberately injected `replay_mismatch` planning failure, and appended one separate feedback event. Event counts were 3 `policy.proposal_observed`, 2 `policy.proposal_result`, 2 `policy.verification_observed`, 1 `policy.proposal_failed` and 1 `policy.owner_feedback_reported`. The script prints measured JSON and saves it beneath a new `.faber/observation-demo/run-*` directory each time; bytes and runtime vary. Its provenance is **fake-development**: this is a synthetic regression demonstration, not an external repository benchmark, detection-rate estimate, training result or payment validation.

Remaining gaps are deliberate: raw partial execution before a `ProofWorkflowResult` returns is unavailable; failures before a planning request exists are outside capture; retrieval provenance, rejected check sets, owner effort, downstream incidents and unobserved alternatives are not fabricated. Capture adds no consent/export workflow, authenticated owner identity, hostile-filesystem isolation or trainer. These observations support a future within-repository comparison against a frontier model with chronological retrieved histories; they do not establish that training improves on that baseline.

## Learning and business implications

For a single firm, consented repeated maintenance can produce useful repository-specific examples: accepted check sets, owner edits and cost/latency tradeoffs under stable conventions. Retrieval plus a frontier model should be the initial system; simple ranking of approved capabilities may become useful once enough **explicit** comparisons exist. Historical imitation may learn conventions, but merged examples alone reward survivorship and may reproduce weak checks. A contextual bandit requires logged action probabilities and safe exploration; offline RL needs richer trajectories, rewards and counterfactual assumptions that this journal does not create.

Cross-repository transfer is uncertain because check IDs, environments, budgets and failure costs vary. Retain interpretable capability descriptions and task/environment features now, but do not normalize every repository into an invented universal action space. Explicitly separate within-repository learning results from transfer results.

The possible data advantage lies in consented, prospective owner decisions and observed consequences that GitHub does not expose, especially rejected proposals and review effort. Mere accumulation of public commits or JSON envelopes is readily reproducible. Technical defensibility remains the binding/enforcement system; a learning moat is conditional on distribution, repeated valuable use, permissions and measurable improvement over frontier-plus-retrieval. Nothing in this audit establishes a model-training business or willingness to pay.
